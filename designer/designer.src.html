Where the schedule comes from

The Inkplate reads a plain-text file from this address and shows one line on the screen for each line of text. Change the file and the screen follows at the next check, with no new export.

Split a line into the same columns as typed entries with | or a tab, e.g. MTWT | 6:40am | In DRL until 1:40pm. A line with two parts puts the first under the days and times; a line with no | runs across the whole width. Start a line with ! to show it in the happening-now ink, or # to leave it out.

Until its first read the Inkplate shows “Loading schedule...”. If a later read fails it keeps the last schedule it got, even through a power cut. It reads up to 4 KB. Checks happen after a screen refresh, so a change shows up at the following minute.

Schedule inks

Schedule entries

Each entry is one line on the screen, in this order: its days, its time, then what it is, for example “MTWT 6:40am In DRL until 1:40pm” or “F All day Work from home”. Entries that span a window or last all day light up while they're happening. Use ↑ ↓ to set the order.

Each photo is resized to 600×448 and dithered to the seven inks. Dithering and boost apply to every photo and can be changed any time.

Clock over photos

With the clock on, photos redraw every minute so the time stays right, which means a refresh flash each minute. Turn it off and each photo stays put, untouched, until its time is up.

Time format

Each name shows from its start time until the next one starts. The screen only redraws when the name changes, at midnight for the date, when a schedule entry starts or ends, and when the weather or schedule is fetched, so it flashes far less often. Raising "Refresh weather every" above cuts the flashing further.

The 6COLOR panel can only redraw the whole screen: each update is a full refresh of about 12–15 seconds with flashing, even when only the time changed. Redrawing less often means less flashing and longer battery life. The clock then changes on the hour and every N minutes after (with 5 minutes: 10:00, 10:05, 10:10…), and the device wakes early so each change shows up on time. Weather and schedule changes show at the next redraw.

Weather inks

Weather location

Weather comes from OpenWeatherMap's free Current Weather API. The clock is set from internet time (NTP) and the time zone comes from the weather reply for your zip code, daylight saving included.

Export for Arduino + microSD
